# Launch-Season Video Ads for Mauritian Gaming Sellers: Pitch Playbook

Prepared 24 October 2026. Launch is Thursday 19 November; pre-load opens Thursday 12 November. That leaves about 26 days of selling, and the store's busiest weeks are the next three.

---

## 1. The real objection, and why it is weaker than it sounds

The worry: "Tech stores know these videos are AI-made, so they'll say they can make them themselves and won't pay."

What is true:
- They *can* open a tool and get a clip. Almost none of them will make twelve clips on schedule, in the right sizes, with a hook in the first second, during the month their staff are busy answering pre-order messages.
- Generic AI clips look like generic AI clips. People scroll past them. Taste, timing and editing are what make one clip sell and another get ignored.
- A store making "GTA-style" content on its own is the one most likely to drop in the GTA logo, Jason and Lucia, trailer footage or a licensed song. Take-Two opposed a Florida sandwich shop's "Vice City Subs" trademark (13 Feb 2026), and its brand-protection agent sent about 300 takedown notices on X in one sweep (Aug–Sep 2026). A takedown in launch week wipes out the store's best-performing post.

So you never sell "a video". You sell **customers in the launch window**, delivered as a ready campaign, that stays clear of Rockstar's lawyers.

### Three rules for talking about how it's made
1. **Don't lead with tools.** Your first message is about their product and their customers. You are a motion designer who produces the work. (This is a fixed rule in your outreach playbook.)
2. **Never deny it.** If they ask "is this AI?", answer plainly: *"Yes, AI is part of my pipeline, like editing software is. You're paying for the idea, the edit, the timing, and a campaign that brings people to your WhatsApp."*
3. **Make the price about output, not hours.** If they say "it only took you an hour", the answer is: *"The hour is quick because I've built the system. You're buying the result, the same way you don't pay a mechanic for how long the bolt took."*

### Objection answers to keep on your phone
| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "We can make this ourselves." | "You can. The question is whether you'll make twelve, on time, in your busiest month. I'll have the first one live by Monday." |
| "It's just AI." | "AI makes clips. I make the ones people stop for, and I keep you clear of the GTA logo and trailer footage, which Rockstar takes down fast." |
| "Too expensive." | "One extra PS5 Pro sale covers the whole pack. If the first video doesn't bring messages, you don't continue." |
| "We'll think about it." | "Of course. The window closes on 19 November, after that it's a normal month. Can I post the free one this week so you can see the numbers?" |
| "We already have an agency." | "Keep them for the brand work. This is only launch content, fast and cheap enough to test." |

## 2. The twist: you as the distribution channel

Your idea, posting the ads on your own page and sending customers to the store, is the strongest part of the offer. It turns you from "someone who makes videos" into "someone who brings buyers". A store can make a clip itself; it cannot get a second audience by itself.

**The catch:** it only works once your page has an audience. So build the page *now*, as the place Mauritians go for the launch.

### The launch hub page
- **What it is:** a TikTok + Instagram + Facebook page that covers the GTA 6 launch for Mauritius. Countdown, "how to set up a US/UK/FR PlayStation account", pre-load reminders for 12 November, deal round-ups from partner stores, launch-night info.
- **Name:** your own brand. No "GTA", "VI", "Vice City" or "Rockstar" in the page name or logo. Text that truthfully says "GTA VI launch" inside posts is normal descriptive use; the logo and artwork are not.
- **Why people follow it:** nobody local is answering the questions Mauritians actually have: which account region to use, where it's in stock, what it costs in rupees, when to pre-load.
- **Content rhythm:** one post a day until launch, two a day in launch week. Countdown Shorts, a weekly "where to buy in Mauritius" round-up, partner spots.

### How the store pays you (pick one per store)
| Model | How it works | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Production only** | You make the spots, they post them. Price anchor about Rs 8,000–15,000 per spot (in line with under-€300 French freelance briefs). | Bigger chains with their own audience. |
| **B. Production + distribution** | Lower production fee, plus the spot runs on your hub page with a tracked link, plus a fee per featured post or a commission per sale. | Small and mid sellers who want customers, not files. |
| **C. Free first spot, then paid** | First spot free, posted on both pages, tracked. If it brings messages, they buy the launch pack. | Your first two partners, to get a real result you can quote. |

### How you prove you sent the customers
- **WhatsApp click-to-chat link with a pre-filled message,** for example *"Bonjour, je viens de [your page], code LAUNCH"*. Every chat that opens with that line came from you.
- **A small customer reward on the code** (free thumb grips, Rs 100 off accessories) so customers want to say it, and the store can't quietly ignore it.
- **Meta Partnership Ads:** the store can pay to boost your post from your handle, labelled "Paid partnership". You need a professional account with branded content tools on; the store gets an ad code from you. This is a documented Meta format and keeps the disclosure correct.
- **Weekly screenshot report:** reach, link clicks, messages with the code.

### Category exclusivity (only offer it if you keep it)
One console retailer, one gaming café, one card/digital seller per launch season. It's honest scarcity, and it stops two partners competing on your page.

## 3. What you sell: the packs

All prices are suggestions anchored on the per-spot range above. Never put a price in the first message; prices come up only after they reply.

| Pack | Contents | Suggested price |
|---|---|---|
| **Launch Spot** | One 15–20 s vertical spot (9:16) of their product in the neon-sunset style, plus a 6 s cut for Stories | Rs 8,000–15,000 |
| **Launch Pack** | Three spots (pre-order push, pre-load reminder 12 Nov, "in stock / launch night" 19 Nov), Story cuts, a posting calendar | About Rs 25,000 |
| **Launch Pack + Hub** | Launch Pack, plus features on your hub page with tracked WhatsApp link and code, weekly report | Launch Pack price, plus a fee per featured post or an agreed commission per tracked sale |

**Creative rules for every spot (IP-safe):** no GTA, VI or Rockstar logo; no Jason, Lucia or other game characters; no trailer or gameplay footage; no songs from the trailers or game; no "Vice City" as a headline or product name. Use the palette (neon pink and teal, palms, sunset grids, chrome type), the store's own products, the store's own name and address.

## 4. Who to pitch, in order

Pitch the businesses where launch customers actually spend:
1. **Independent game sellers** (WhatsApp/Instagram shops): fastest decisions, launch is their biggest month. Researched below.
2. **Gaming cafés and esports venues**: launch-night events need a promo video. Researched below.
3. **Electronics retailers with PS5/PS5 Pro stock**: bigger budgets, slower decisions. Researched below.
4. **Card and digital-code sellers** (US/UK/FR PSN cards): Ultimate Edition is digital-only, so they get the launch traffic.
5. **Non-gaming brands riding the hype**: car dealers, burger joints, nightclubs, gyms. The "neon sunset" look worked for Hyundai, BMW and Burger King abroad; no Mauritian brand has done it yet.

Do **not** promote any product that breaks Sony's terms, such as "shared account" PS5 games. If a partner sells those, only advertise their legitimate stock.
